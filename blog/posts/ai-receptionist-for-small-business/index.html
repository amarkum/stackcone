What an AI receptionist actually does

An AI receptionist is an AI voice agent set up to do a front-desk job. A good one will:

  • Answer every call instantly — nights, weekends, and when your team is busy
  • Answer common questions (hours, pricing, services, location, insurance)
  • Book, reschedule and cancel appointments directly in your calendar or practice software
  • Capture new-lead details and send them to your CRM
  • Text the caller a booking link, directions, or intake form
  • Recognise urgent calls and transfer them to a person
  • Send you a summary of every call

It will not (and should not) give medical, legal or financial advice, handle payment card numbers by voice, or pretend to be human.

Off-the-shelf AI receptionist apps start around $50–500 per month. A custom AI receptionist — built around your exact booking software, services and rules — typically costs $10k–25k to build plus usage (roughly $0.08–0.30 per minute). Custom makes sense when the apps can’t connect to your system, or when calls involve real decisions (triage, qualification, quoting).

Is it worth it? A simple ROI example

Take a dental clinic that gets 1,200 calls a month and misses 20% of them.

  • 240 missed calls × 25% that would have booked = 60 lost appointments
  • 60 × $150 average first-visit value = $9,000 per month in missed revenue
  • AI receptionist usage: 1,200 calls × 3 min × ~$0.20 = ~$720 per month

Even recovering a third of those missed bookings pays for a custom build in a few months. Run the same numbers with your own call volume, miss rate and booking value.

A typical AI receptionist call flow — answer, act, and escalate the rest

  1. List your top call reasons and the right response for each.
  2. Collect your business info: services, prices, hours, policies, FAQs.
  3. Connect your calendar or booking system so it books real slots.
  4. Choose escalation rules: which calls transfer, which become callbacks.
  5. Port or forward your number — start with after-hours only if you’re nervous.
  6. Test 30–50 calls yourself and with friends. Fix the script.
  7. Go live, then review transcripts weekly for the first month.

Common mistakes

  • No escape hatch: callers who can’t reach a person hang up angry. Always offer a transfer or callback.
  • Reading long info aloud: text addresses and links instead.
  • Double bookings: the agent must book through your real system, not a copy of it.
  • Hiding that it’s AI: disclose it. It builds trust and keeps you compliant.
  • Set and forget: a 15-minute weekly transcript review keeps quality high.

FAQ

How much does an AI receptionist cost?

Off-the-shelf AI receptionist apps cost roughly $50–500 per month. A custom AI receptionist integrated with your booking system usually costs $10k–25k to build plus about $0.08–0.30 per minute of calls.

Can an AI receptionist book appointments?

Yes. When connected to your calendar, practice management or booking software, an AI receptionist checks availability, books, reschedules and cancels appointments, and texts confirmations.

Is an AI receptionist better than an answering service?

For routine calls, usually yes: it answers instantly, handles unlimited simultaneous calls, and books directly into your system. Human answering services still do better with unusual or sensitive calls, so the best setups escalate those to people.

Is an AI receptionist legal?

Yes, when you disclose that callers are speaking to AI, follow call-recording consent rules, and protect personal data (HIPAA in US healthcare, GDPR in the UK and EU).
