What is an AI agent (in plain English)?

An AI agent is software that uses a large language model (LLM) to understand a request, decide what to do, and then do it — by calling your systems, not just replying with text.

A chatbot tells a customer your refund policy. An agent looks up their order, checks the policy, issues the refund in Stripe, updates the ticket in Zendesk, and only asks a human when the refund is above your limit.

How to pick your first AI agent

The first agent should be boring, frequent, and measurable. Score each candidate use case:

  • Volume: Does it happen hundreds of times a month? (Calls, tickets, leads, invoices.)
  • Repetition: Are 70%+ of cases variations of the same 10–20 questions or tasks?
  • Data access: Do the answers live in documents or systems with an API?
  • Low blast radius: If the agent gets one wrong, can a human fix it easily?
  • Clear metric: Can you count resolution rate, bookings, or hours saved?

Avoid starting with “an agent that runs the whole department”. Multi-agent systems are real, but they are a second or third project, not the first.

A 30-day plan to launch your first agent

Ship one narrow agent in a month, measure it, then expand

  1. Week 1: Choose the use case. Export 100–300 real examples (calls, tickets, emails) with the right answer or action.
  2. Week 2: Connect the knowledge sources and the few tools the agent needs. Read-only first.
  3. Week 3: Run the agent against your examples. Measure accuracy. Fix missing content before tweaking prompts.
  4. Week 4: Shadow mode — the agent drafts, a human approves. Then switch on auto-resolve for the cases it gets right.

Risks and how to avoid them

  • Hallucinations: Ground every answer in your documents and require citations; let the agent say “I don’t know”.
  • Wrong actions: Give tools the minimum permissions, set limits (e.g. refunds under $50), and log every action.
  • Privacy: Redact personal data from logs; for UK/EU customers, host in-region and sign a DPA (see our GDPR guide for AI agents).
  • No owner: Assign someone to review the dashboard weekly. Agents improve when failures are fed back.

FAQ

What is an AI agent for business?

An AI agent is software that uses a large language model to understand a request, decide what to do, and act through your business systems — such as your CRM, helpdesk, calendar or billing — escalating to a human when unsure.

How much does it cost to build an AI agent?

A focused pilot typically costs $8k–25k over 3–6 weeks, with $200–1,000 per month in running costs. Production multi-channel agents usually cost $25k–80k to build.

What is the best first AI agent for a small business?

Usually an AI receptionist or a customer support agent: both have high volume, repetitive requests, and a clear metric (calls answered, tickets resolved).

Will AI agents replace my staff?

In practice they remove repetitive work — the same 20 questions, data entry, scheduling — so staff handle the complex, high-value cases. Most businesses redeploy people rather than cut them.
