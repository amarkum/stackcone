The AI Bubble Debate

September 2026 · Published by Amar Kumar

Capex is enormous. HBM is tight. Power interconnects slip. Model labs still raise. Those facts can support a boom and a bubble at the same time. The useful question is not “is AI a bubble?” It is “which cash flows survive if the slides are wrong?”

This is a builder’s cut of that debate: what the skeptics get right, what the buildout still has to finish, and a simple test for a product team that does not want to underwrite someone else’s GPU order.

Both stories can be true

Search interest in “AI bubble” rose next to NVIDIA and DRAM. That is not noise. It is people stress-testing a story that ate most of the last two years of tech capex.

Bull caseBear case
Inference demand is still growingTraining clusters were front-loaded
HBM and power are scarce because use is realScarcity can be over-ordering
Agents need more tokens per taskTokens per dollar may not cover depreciation
Software is earlySoftware may never need this much silicon

What skeptics get right

  • Depreciation clocks. A GPU that ships with less HBM than last year’s slide still has to earn back the rack.
  • Utilization. Peak reserved capacity is not the same as tokens sold.
  • Power and interconnect slip dates even when the chip exists.
  • Application revenue still lags infrastructure spend at most companies.

A thinner Rubin Ultra HBM SKU is not proof demand vanished. It is proof the bill of materials is the scarce resource. That is the memory wall and the DRAM supercycle in one sentence.

What the buildout still is

Hyperscalers are still buying. Server DRAM LTAs run for years. That is not a meme-stock chart. It is a procurement process that is painful to unwind. The risk is not “nobody uses models.” The risk is “too much of the same model, at the wrong memory and power point, for workloads that fit a smaller card.”

Indicative split of attention: infra spend is loud. Application gross margin is quieter. That gap is the debate.

A product test

Would this product survive a 40% GPU price shock?

Toggle the constraints. If the product only works on cheap, abundant HBM, you are underwriting the bubble.

Cut context, rerank, and route to a resident model. If quality collapses, the product was a capacity trade.

What to build anyway

  • Eval harnesses and golden sets — agent testing
  • Routing and cheaper models — economical RAG models
  • Retrieval that earns its tokens — RAG evaluation

Those layers stay useful if the boom continues and if it compresses. A bubble-proof stack is just a cost-aware stack.

FAQ

Is this financial advice?

No. It is a product-architecture read of a public debate.

Does a memory shortage prove there is no bubble?

No. Shortages can be real demand or synchronized over-ordering. They do prove FLOPS are not free.

What should a startup do?

Design for 192 GB SKUs, expensive DIMMs, and higher token prices. If the product still works, you are not betting the company on a slide.
